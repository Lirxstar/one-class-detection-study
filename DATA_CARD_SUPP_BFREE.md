## Encoding history

- JPEG test: an image is flagged if its lattice on the aligned grid exceeds the median over the 64 grid offsets by more than 0.1. Stored images flagged: REAL 0, SD35 0, FLUX 0. Positive controls (the same images re-encoded once, 4:4:4) detected: Q90 92.7%; Q96 94.7%. The best of the 64 offsets is the aligned grid for 95.3% of controls but for 2.0% (REAL), 1.1% (SD35), 3.1% (FLUX) of stored images (chance 1.6%). No JPEG history at the stored resolution: **True**. The test misses a few controls whose content gives a high lattice level at every offset, so it bounds rather than excludes a JPEG history in single images; it cannot see a JPEG applied before the real images were resized.
- Real images: RAISE photographs (developed from camera RAW) downscaled to about 1024^2 pixels (1258 x 833 or 1256 x 835, i.e. the camera aspect ratio at the pixel count of 1024 x 1024), then stored as PNG. They carry a resampling history that the synthetic images (native 1024 x 1024 generator output) do not.
- All files are lossless 8-bit RGB PNG; the JPEG-versus-PNG asymmetry of the core study is absent.

## Colour space

- Rule (fixed before feature extraction): every ICC-tagged real image is converted to standard sRGB with ImageCms (relative colorimetric, LittleCMS 2.19); the untagged real image is assumed sRGB and left unchanged; synthetic images have no ICC chunk and are unchanged. All 1,000 source ids are kept (primary analysis); the sRGB-tagged ids form the sensitivity analysis.
- `Nikon Adobe RGB 4.0.0.3001` (256 images): conversion leaves 0 images bitwise unchanged; largest change 144 levels, median per-image mean change 4.314.
- `Nikon sRGB 4.0.0.3002` (743 images): conversion leaves 743 images bitwise unchanged; largest change 0 levels, median per-image mean change 0.000.
- Without conversion, Adobe RGB values read as sRGB are desaturated, a real-only pixel difference. The conversion removes it but is itself a processing step applied to real images only (identity for the sRGB-tagged ones: True), hence the sensitivity analysis without converted images.
- File-level cues remain: real and synthetic size sets disjoint: True; ICC chunk only in real files: True. The visual methods see only decoded 224 x 224 pixels, never the file size or chunks; any metadata classifier would separate the classes perfectly.

## Remaining differences (limitations)

- Resampling history (real downscaled, synthetic native), aspect ratio (3:2 versus 1:1), and the semantic, not pixel-level, content match (synthetic images are more stylized). The native-crop condition removes resizing by the pipeline but not the real images' earlier downscaling.
